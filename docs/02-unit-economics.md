## 1. The funnel (base case)

```
 100 raw leads (R120 ad cost each + R3 AI/messaging)
  │  40% pass AI qualification
  ▼
  40 qualified
  │  60% book an appointment
  ▼
  24 booked
  │  80% show up
  ▼
 19.2 attended  ──►  billed at R1,200 each
```

**5.2 raw leads per attended appointment → R641 fully loaded cost → R559 contribution margin (47%).**

### What the board should take from this

1. **Pricing power is the biggest lever.** Never discount the per-appointment price. Discount the *platform fee* instead if a deal needs a sweetener. Territory Lock exists so we can raise the effective price without changing the per-appointment headline.
2. **The three funnel rates are worth the same.** A 20% loss in qualification, booking or show-up costs the same amount, because they multiply. Show rate is the cheapest to fix (reminders cost cents), so it gets engineering priority first.
3. **Ad cost matters least of the five.** That's counter-intuitive. Most operators obsess over CPL, but conversion quality after the click matters more.
4. **The business is binary in the conservative case.** At ~9.5 raw leads per appointment, every appointment loses R793. That's why the plan has a hard **kill switch at R950 cost per attended appointment** and a 60-day go/no-go gate. Without those rules, this business can burn cash quickly.

## 4. Cash flow and working capital

Ad spend is paid daily, up front, to Meta and Google. Revenue arrives after the appointment happens. In the base case at month 12 that is **~R234k/month of ad spend running ahead of revenue**.

The fix is structural: **prepaid appointment credit.**

- Each client tops up at least 10 appointments (R12,000) in advance.
- Ads for a client pause automatically when their credit covers less than 1 week of expected appointments.
- Result: clients fund the ad spend, so growth doesn't need debt or outside capital.

This rule is enforced in the data model (`clients.prepaid_credit` in [`db/migrations/0001_init.sql`](../db/migrations/0001_init.sql)).
